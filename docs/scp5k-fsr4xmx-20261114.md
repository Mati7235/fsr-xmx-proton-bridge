# SCP: 5K with FSR 4.1.2 XMX (2026-11-14)

**Working result in a match, not just injection.** SCP: 5K (UE 4.27.2,
D3D12) renders a PvP match with the FSR 4.1.2 XMX mod, OptiScaler and the
candidate Mesa driver on Intel Arc B580. The user confirmed the visual result;
the local capture of the PvP session shows the correct scene.

It is still not a bit-exact validation of FSR: there is no independent oracle
and no deterministic readback of the PRE. See [What this proves and what it does not](#what-this-proves-and-what-it-does-not).

## What this proves and what it does not

What it proves:

- The 4.1.2 mod loaded, verified the weights in `fsr-original.dll`, and built and
  injected `Xe2 SIMD16-DPAS` kernels.
- The sampler heap was found, 244 CM were injected and 99 FSR4 resources were
  detected; 236 PSO lines with `hr=0`; 0 device lost.
- The PvP scene rendered correctly with the upscaler active (user).

What it does not prove:

- Mathematical accuracy or equivalence with reference FSR 4 (no oracle).
- Deterministic readback of the input/output images.
- Measured quality or FPS; a single session.
- That the overlay label shows the correct model (it may say `4.0.2`
  without `FSR4_XMX_PROVIDER_INDEX`).
- Online/EAC stability: it is a multiplayer game; use single-player or accept
  the risk.

## Rollback

Manifest: `analysis/scp5k-fsr4xmx-20261114/manifest.txt`.

- Restore the official `amd_fidelityfx_upscaler_dx12.dll` from `backup/`.
- Delete `fsr-original.dll`, `cabinet.dll` and `igdext64.dll`.
- Restore `OptiScaler.ini` from `backup/`.
- Stop the unit `scp5k-fsr4xmx-20261114c`.
- The kernels stay in `~/.cache/xess-xmx/kernels/`; delete that cache if you
  want to clean up. `ANV_CM_KERNEL_DIR` was not used in this run.
